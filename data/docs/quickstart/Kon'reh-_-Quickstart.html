Kon'reh — Quickstart

10-minute read No notation required

This page teaches you to play Kon'reh — nothing more. For tournament notation, timing edge cases, AI-opponent "Schools," and the full strategy guide, see the Definitive Guide.

1. What You're Playing

Kon'reh is a two-player abstract strategy game on an 8×8 board. Think of it as a chess cousin: you're trying to capture your opponent's king piece (called Blue) — but capturing it doesn't end the game outright. It kicks off a race to plant a banner in their home corner before they can bring their Blue back into play.

2. Setup

Each side has 4 piece types, set up in rows from your home corner (the Home Apex) outward:

RowPieces
Row 1Blue (your king) on the Home Apex
Row 22 Oranges
Row 3Red — Green — Red
Row 44 Reds

The opponent mirrors this from the opposite corner. Players alternate turns, one move each — except the second player, who gets two moves (not the same piece twice) on their very first turn to balance going second.

3. How Pieces Move

Every square connects diagonally in four directions: two lanes heading toward the opponent ("onward") and two heading back toward you ("homeward"). Pieces slide along one straight diagonal lane — no turning mid-move — and can't jump over other pieces (except Blue's special moves, below).

PieceMoves Onward (exact)Moves Homeward (up to)
Red2 squares1 square
Orange3 squares2 squares
Green4 squares3 squares
Blue5 squares4 squares

Capturing: move onto a square with an enemy piece to remove it.

Zone of Control (the one tricky rule): every piece "threatens" the four diagonal squares right next to it. You can move into a threatened square (even to capture), but doing so immediately ends your move — you can't slide through a threatened square to go further.

4. Blue's Special Moves

Your Blue gets two one-time special captures per "life" (used up until Blue is captured and returns):

  • Displacement — step onto an adjacent enemy and remove it.
  • Hop — jump over one adjacent enemy (landing on the empty square just past it) and remove the enemy you jumped.

Use both specials in the same life and your Blue becomes Rooted (frozen) until your next turn. It can still be captured while Rooted.

5. Making More Pieces (Seeding)

Your two side corners are your Sanctums. If your Blue ends a move on one Sanctum, you may create a new Green piece on the opposite Sanctum (up to 6 Greens total on the board, shared between both players). This also Roots your Blue until your next turn. You can't Seed the very first time your Blue leaves home in a given life.

6. The Center Squares

The four squares in the exact middle of the board are "the Cross." Your Blue can enter it freely, but can only stay there for 3 of your own turns before it must leave — and once it leaves, it can't come back for 2 more turns. This stops one player from camping the center indefinitely.

7. How You Win

1. Capture the enemy Blue. Nothing else ends the game outright.
2. That starts a 5-turn countdown for the losing side to "plant a banner": get any one of their pieces onto your Home Apex.
3. If they plant in time, their Blue returns to play (with both specials refreshed) and the game continues.
4. If the countdown runs out first, they lose. A player can also concede at any time.
The one thing to remember: capturing Blue isn't the finish line, it's the start of a chase. Before you go for the capture, make sure you can also stop (or outrace) their banner run — otherwise you've just handed them a fresh Blue and lost tempo.
