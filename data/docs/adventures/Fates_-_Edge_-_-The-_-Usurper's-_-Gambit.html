👑 The Usurper's Gambit

Tier III 4-5 Sessions Court Intrigue Legitimacy vs. Popularity Author: Nicholas A. Gasper

Ecktoria, heir to the shattered Utaran Empire, teeters on the edge of civil war. A charismatic pretender, Duke Logan the Bold, has won the militia's loyalty and begun invoking the ancient rites that would claim the Mandate of Everflame. The recognized heir, King Aldric the Lame, clings to power through law, tradition, and the blessing of a wavering High Temple. The party must move through marble forums, sealed archives, and a besieged basilica to decide who — if anyone — deserves the crown, in a city where legitimacy is manufactured as often as it is inherited.

"Every mosaic in this city is a political statement. Every sash color is a declaration of war. You want to know who rules Ecktoria? Don't ask the throne. Ask the tile-setters." — Grand Magistrate Valeria Thorne

Reading an encounter badge. Each badge names a DV and a Position. It does not name a dice pool, and that is deliberate: the pool is Attribute + Skill, chosen from how the player says they are going about it. Two characters can attempt the same encounter with different pools and both be right. Where an approach is obviously the intended one, the badge says so; where it does not, ask what they are doing and build the pool from the answer.

🗒️ GM Notes

Act I: Establish Ecktoria as a place where ceremony IS politics. Let the party's reaction to the Envoy's humiliation set their initial reputation.
Act II: The genealogical puzzle is the mechanical centerpiece — both claims are real but compromised. This is the natural, sad consequence of an empire quietly cutting corners for generations, not a shocking betrayal.
Act III: Every relationship or piece of leverage from earlier acts should matter here. Track who owes the party what.
Act IV: If the party has built a strong case, let mechanics reflect they've effectively already won — this act is about whether the city accepts that victory. Reward creative third-path solutions generously.
Forbidden early revelations: Don't reveal both claims are flawed before Act II's archive discovery; keep Thorne evasive about her knowledge until trust is earned; keep Dominicus's private decision hidden until Act IV; let the Silent Opposition's role surface through investigation, not exposition.
