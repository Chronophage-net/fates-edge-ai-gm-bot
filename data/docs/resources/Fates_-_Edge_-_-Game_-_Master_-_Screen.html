The Philosophy of Consequence

Narrative First

Every roll tells a story. In Fate's Edge, mechanics serve the story. When you roll dice, you are not asking "did I succeed?"—you are asking "what happens next?"

The Golden Rule: When in doubt, make the choice that serves the story. Set DV=3, Position=Controlled, and let the dice fall.

Safety at the Table

Fate's Edge explores mature themes. Use these tools to keep play safe:

  • X-Card: Any player may say "X" to stop a scene. No questions asked.
  • Lines: Content that will not appear (e.g., "no harm to children").
  • Veils: Content that fades to black (e.g., "romance is implied, not described").

Establish these in Session Zero.

The Core Resolution

The 90-Second Loop

Every meaningful action follows this cycle:

  1. Declare your intent and approach (Attribute + Skill).
  2. GM sets DV (2–5+) and Position (Dominant/Controlled/Desperate).
  3. Roll dice pool (Attribute + Skill d10s).
  4. Count successes (6–10 = 1 success). Each 1 = Story Beat (SB) for GM.
  5. Apply outcome using the Outcome Matrix.
  6. GM spends SB to introduce complications.

Difficulty Value (DV)

DVWhen to Use
2Routine — clear path, no pressure
3Pressured — mild opposition (default)
4Hard — serious resistance
5+Extreme — dramatic gamble

Default DV is 3. Do not inflate DVs to "challenge" players—use Position instead.

Position

PositionFrameMechanical Effect
DominantYou press advantageRe-roll one failure
ControlledBalanced normNo re-rolls
DesperateAct under duressRe-roll one success

Pro tip: Spend 1 Boon to improve Position by one step before rolling.

The Outcome Matrix

Roll ResultWhat Happens
Successes ≥ DV, SB = 0Clean Success — Intent achieved, no complication.
Successes ≥ DV, SB > 0Success with SB — Intent achieved; GM spends SB.
0 < Successes < DVPartial — Progress; gain 1 Boon.
Successes = 0Miss — No progress; gain 2 Boons; GM spends SB.

A Partial is not failure. It moves the story forward: improve Position for next attempt, reduce DV, succeed at reduced scale, or reveal information.

A Miss is never "nothing happens." The GM must introduce a complication.

Critical Success (10s)

A roll of 10 counts as two successes. If the overall roll succeeds:

  • 1 ten: Strong success—improve Position or gain minor advantage.
  • 2 tens: Exceptional—choose two benefits.
  • 3+ tens: Legendary—decisive victory; clear 1 segment from a relevant timer.

Resources: Boons & Story Beats

Boons — Player Currency

Boons represent luck, momentum, and narrative leverage. Spend them freely.

Earning BoonsGainSpending Boons
Partial successGain 1Re-roll a die (1 Boon)
MissGain 2Improve Position (1 Boon)
Bond-driven actionGain 1 (once/bond/session)Activate Asset (1 Boon)
GM award+1 (rare)Convert 2 → 1 XP (once/session)

Holding cap: 5 Boons. At end of scene, reduce to 2 (excess lost).

The Math of Fun

Hoarding Boons for XP conversion starves the table of drama. A re-rolled failure or an improved Position creates a memorable moment. Spend Boons!

Story Beats (SB) — GM Currency

Every time a player rolls a 1, the GM gains Story Beats. Spend SB to introduce complications:

CostComplication
1 SBMinor — noise, distraction, tick a timer +1
2 SBModerate — alarm raised, lose advantage, worsen Position
3+ SBMajor — reinforcements, scene shifts, break an asset

Be aggressive with SB. Unused pressure is wasted momentum.

Injury & Exhaustion

Fatigue — The Weight of the World

Each point of Fatigue worsens your Position. If already Desperate, each Fatigue imposes −1 die instead.

Fatigue LevelEffect
1Position worsens one step
2If already Desperate, −1 die; otherwise Position worsens
3−2 dice (if already Desperate from Fatigue)
4+Collapse, KO, incapacitated

Recovery: Short rest removes 2 Fatigue; full night's rest removes all.

Harm — Serious Injury

Harm LevelEffect
Harm 1−1 die on related actions
Harm 2−1 dice on most actions
Harm 3Incapacitated or dying

The Roller-Coaster Effect

Taking Harm clears Fatigue. The shock of a wound resets your exhaustion. This creates dramatic swings—a character near collapse who takes a wound and finds a second wind.

Building Your Character

10-Minute Checklist

Start with 32 XP (max 36 with Bonds/Complications).

  1. Concept: One sentence (origin, profession, defining trait).
  2. Attributes (1–5): Spend XP—primary to 3 (9 XP), secondaries to 2 (6 XP each).
  3. Skills (0–5): Spend 8–12 XP on key skills.
  4. Talents: 1–3 talents (2–6 XP each).
  5. Patron (optional): Choose a Patron for magic.
  6. Assets/Followers (optional): Minor Asset (4 XP) or follower (Cap² XP).
  7. Bonds & Complications: Up to +4 XP total.

The Known World: Acasia

"The Broken Province"

Genre & Mood

Low fantasy / mercenary realism / border feud. Acasia festers between the fallen Utaran Empire and the ambitions of petty kings. Every bridge demands a price, and every road curves back to the same cursed crossroads.

What You Notice First

The smell of mildew on old parchment and wet ash. The way every milestone has been recarved. The silence of a village that heard you coming and chose not to open its gates.

The One Rule That Kills Newcomers

Never sign a contract on a bridge. The river is a witness, and it never forgets a signature.

Key Factions

FactionLeaderGoal
Broken MarchMargravine MiraKeep the province from fracturing
Lame King's ClaimantsOren "Tin Crown"Be recognized as true sovereign
Free CompaniesVarious captainsFind permanent contracts
Blackwood MatriarchsHedge-witchesSurvive the Curse

The Curse of Acasia

The land remembers what we did—and what we failed to do. Every broken milestone is a forgotten promise.

Mechanical effect: When you cross a bridge or crossroads, roll Spirit + Resolve (DV 3). On a failure, the Curse stirs—gain 1 Fatigue or a minor complication (GM's choice).

Acasia Mini-Generator (d6)

  1. A bridge toll has tripled overnight; the toll-keeper is a deserter from your old company.
  2. A hedge-witch offers to break your blood-debt—for the memory of your first kill.
  3. The Lame King travels with a mute jester who writes prophecies on fallen leaves.
  4. Bandits stole a Blackwood Matriarch's curse-box. Recover it before it opens.
  5. A free company captain wants to hire you—but their last three hires died of "accidents."
  6. The Margravine's seal is missing. Whoever finds it can rewrite border law.

Running Fate's Edge

The GM Mindset

You are not a referee. You are a co-creator of drama. Your tools (SB, timers) are how you play the game.

Your Defaults

When in doubt:

  • DV: 3
  • Position: Controlled
  • Consequence: Fatigue, time pressure, or exposure
  • Timers: One short timer tied to the scene

The Three-Timer Screneline

Keep 1–3 active timers per scene:

SegmentsWhen to Use
4Short, straightforward challenge
6Standard scene or tension
8Extended or complex problem
10Epic or campaign-long pressure

GM Survival Tips

When Players Go Off the Rails

Do not panic. Ask: "What is your goal?" Name the simplest obstacle—then set Position and DV.

When Players Hoard Boons

Remind them: "Boons are not XP tokens—they are dramatic fuel." Introduce time pressure.

When a Scene Stalls

Ask a leading question, tick a timer, or cut to another player.

When You Don't Know a Rule

Make a ruling that keeps the story moving. Default to DV 3, Controlled Position. Look it up later.

The Commando GM

"You can run Fate's Edge with zero prep. Here's how."

The Core Workflow

Opening Scene:

  1. Draw Spade = Place, Heart = Actor, Club = Pressure
  2. Ask: "How does the Pressure make the Actor's life harder?"
  3. Write one sentence. Set a Timer based on the highest rank.

Player Action:

  1. Declare intent. Set DV = 3, Position = Controlled (unless fiction demands otherwise).
  2. Player rolls.
  3. On a 1: You get SB. Spend it. Tick a timer, worsen Position, or introduce a twist.
  4. On a Partial/Miss: Player gains Boons. Introduce a complication. Draw a Club if stuck.

Scene Resolution:

  1. When the Timer fills, the scene resolves.
  2. Victory, defeat, or a new, more complicated situation.
The Commando GM's Mantra: No prep. No stress. Just reacting to dice and cards.

Zero-Prep Scene Generation

Draw three cards from a standard deck:

DrawElementQuestion to Ask

Synthesis Question: "How does the Pressure make the Actor's life harder?" The answer is your opening hook.

The Deck of Consequences

When you don't know what happens next, draw a card.

Suit Interpretation:

  • Hearts: "Who is connected? What bond is at stake?"
  • Spades: "What breaks? Who gets hurt?"
  • Clubs: "What runs out? What must be sacrificed?"
  • Diamonds: "What is hidden? What truth is revealed?"

Three-Card Synthesis: Draw three cards and force them into one sentence. If a card makes zero sense after 10 seconds, discard it and draw again.

The Lantern at Dusk

A Starter Adventure in Acasia

Setup

The party is hired by Elder Sarai of Duskwood. A blight is spreading from the old barrow, where a cursed lantern was buried. The lantern must be brought to a stone circle to break the curse.

What the Party Knows

  • The lantern is in the central chamber of the barrow.
  • The barrow is guarded by restless spirits (the Duskwights).
  • A rival treasure hunter, Quick Lena, has also entered the barrow.
  • The curse will kill the village's crops in three days.

Timers

ClockEffect
Barrow Collapse [6]Ticks on failure inside. At 6, entrance seals.
Lena's Agenda [4]Ticks each scene inside. At 4, Lena takes the lantern first.

Scenes

Scene 1 — The Entry (DV 3, Controlled)

Obstacle: Cave-in blocks the way (Body + Athletics).

  • Partial: Tick Barrow Collapse +1.
  • Miss: Mark Harm 1, tick Collapse +1.

Scene 2 — The Spirit Corridor (DV 3, Controlled)

Three Duskwights demand an offering (a memory or a secret). Roll Presence + Sway.

  • Partial: They also steal random equipment.
  • Miss: They attack (Cap 2, Harm 1 each).

Scene 3 — The Lantern Chamber

Quick Lena tries to pry the lantern loose. Confront her: Presence + Sway (DV 3).

  • Partial: She agrees to split the reward but demands a later favor (start Debt Timer [4]).

Scene 4 — Escape (Skill Challenge)

The party needs 3 successes before 2 failures to escape. DV 3, Position Desperate. Each failure ticks Barrow Collapse +1.

Resolution

Return the lantern to the stone circle. Break the curse with Spirit + Lore (DV 2).

  • Partial: Curse lifts, but the lantern attracts another danger soon.

Advancement

Each PC earns 6–10 XP at the end.

Runkeeper — The Reliable Path

Requirements: Familiar (Thiasos, 2 XP) + Codex (4 XP) + Patron.

Invoking a Rite: 1 action. Mark +1 Obligation. Effect works as written.

Push It: Once per scene, mark +1 Obligation to amplify (+1 die or +1 Effect).

Obligation Capacity: Spirit + Presence. Exceeding capacity = 1 Fatigue per segment.

Kestra, Traveler's Runkeeper (36 XP)
  • Spirit 3 (9 XP), Wits 2 (6 XP)
  • Lore 2 (6 XP), Insight 1 (2 XP), Sway 1 (2 XP)
  • Talents: Familiar (2 XP), Codex (4 XP)
  • Rites: Road-Sense (4 XP), Traveler's Boon (5 XP)
  • Obligation Capacity: 4 (Spirit 3 + Presence 1)

Free Casting — The TAGS System

For improvisational magic, construct spells using TAGS:

DV = 1 + number of TAGS

Dangerous TAGS (Leap, Fold, Gate, Transform, Dominate) add +2 DV.

Example Spells

  • Light [Illuminate] — DV 2. Create small, sustained light.
  • HEAL [HEAL] — DV 2. Remove 1 Fatigue from self or touched ally.
  • Shield of Air [WARD] [DEFEND] — DV 3. Gain Position +1 on next defense.
  • Flame Cloak [BURNING] [AREA] — DV 3. Enemies suffer −1 die to approach.

Backlash: On a Miss or when GM spends SB, magic backfires—Fatigue 1, Harm 1, or a thematic complication.

GM Advice — Print-Out

Core Principles

  1. You are not a referee. You are a co-creator of drama.
  2. Narrative first. Mechanics serve the story, not the other way around.
  3. Fail forward. A Miss is never "nothing happens." Always escalate.
  4. Spend SB early and often. Unused pressure is wasted momentum.
  5. Set the DV low. Default to 3. Use Position and Timers for tension.

The GM's Defaults

  • DV: 3
  • Position: Controlled
  • Consequence: Fatigue, time pressure, or exposure
  • Timers: One short timer per scene

The Three-Timer Rule

Keep 1–3 active timers per scene:

  1. Situation Timer — Immediate goal (persuasion, escape)
  2. Counter-Clock — Opposition pressure (guards, ritual)
  3. Campaign Timer — Long-term front (faction, curse)

When Players Go Off the Rails

  1. Do not panic.
  2. Ask: "What is your goal?"
  3. Name the simplest obstacle.
  4. Set Position and DV.
  5. Let the dice decide.

When You Don't Know the Rules

  • Make a ruling that keeps the story moving.
  • Default to DV 3, Controlled Position.
  • Look it up after the session.
  • The story is more important than the rulebook.

Quick Reference Tables

Combat in Brief

  • Turn: 1 Action + 1 Move
  • Move: Shift one range band (Close ↔ Near ↔ Far)
  • Opportunity Attack: Leaving Close range without Disengage provokes one free melee attack.
  • Sidestep Strike: Move one band as part of melee attack; worsen Position by one step.
